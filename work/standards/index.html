Decisions

Decision 01 / 05

One layout: the split-screen workbench

Creation flows had drifted into split-screen workbenches, single-column cards, and card grids. I set the split-screen workbench as the standard: a scrollable form on one side and a sticky live preview on the other, with a variant switcher, a summary tab in place of the old review page, and inline errors on any tab with issues.

A shared layout lets a new surface feel familiar on day one.Design principle, layout standardization
Decision 02 / 05

A rubric for steppers versus tabs

Steppers for strictly linear flows, tabs for save-as-draft flows where users move between sections freely. Usage data grounded it: one flow forced every user through a multi-step stepper even though 60 percent created a single item.

Teams carry it forward as their default reasoning, so the debate doesn’t restart on every flow.

Decision 03 / 05

The design file is the source of truth

Every ticket now carries three things: specific affected paths, explicit expected behavior, and a design reference, with the reasoning behind each decision documented alongside the work.

Why: decisions had lived in scattered chat threads, so the same questions came back at every handoff.

Decision 04 / 05

Gates built from an audit, not a template

Design, research, and content kept entering after scope was set. I audited handoff against real projects, from meeting transcripts to tickets to shipped work, and named five recurring failure patterns.

That audit became three gates (discover, plan, build) with entry criteria and named owners, plus a biweekly design and engineering sync where in-flight work is reviewed while it can still change.

Decision 05 / 05

Give the roadmap one demand picture

Demand arrived through three channels, each with its own bias: a sales request log, a research repository, and meeting notes. I used Claude to code 340 logged requests from an 18-month window into weighted themes, then cross-read them against the other two channels across 20+ enterprise accounts.

Themes confirmed by all three channels became high-confidence priorities. Roles and permissions is the current example.
