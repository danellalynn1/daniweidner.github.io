Research and prototype / Enterprise administration

Scoping enterprise access to the folder, not the account

Enterprise customers were working with agencies and contractors on a platform where access was all or nothing. I led research with 15 participants across 10 organizations, defined a folder-level role model, and tested a working admin console before engineering committed.

In one minute

SituationCustomers rarely filed access complaints, so the gap looked small. The real signal was in workarounds: admin access handed out too broadly, and in some cases shared credentials.
What I didRan the study end to end, ranked what surfaced into three problems, defined roles per folder with a hard line around external users, and built the admin flows as a working prototype.
OutcomeA tested direction that de-risks the engineering investment. A roadmap reprioritization paused the build.

Prototype. Invite, assign a role per folder, check what an external partner can reach, transfer or remove. Fictional workspace and people. Open full screen ↗

Decisions

Decision 01 / 03

Look for the risk nobody reported

I owned the study from recruiting and the discussion guide through interviews, surveys, card sorting, prototype sessions, and synthesis. Participants included the legal and compliance reviewers who sign off on access.

Ranking by frequency, severity, and strategic impact left three problems: no resource-level control, no line between internal and external users, and onboarding and offboarding that ran on memory.

No blocker to report is not the same as no risk. The workaround never shows up in the feedback.Research finding, cross-study synthesis
Decision 02 / 03

Attach roles to folders, not the account

Admin controls people and settings, Editor manages content, Viewer reads. External users are a separate class that sees only what’s explicitly shared, so least privilege is the default instead of a cleanup task.

Tradeoff: custom roles, approval flows, and SCIM provisioning all had real demand. I recommended holding them so V1 closed the highest-risk gaps at a scope an early release could carry, and product adopted the recommendation.

Decision 03 / 03

Test the admin lifecycle before engineering does

Permissions mistakes are expensive after launch, because the failure is someone seeing what they shouldn’t. I built invite, change role, reassign, and offboard as a working prototype in Figma Make, and tested it with customers and with people outside the customer base.
